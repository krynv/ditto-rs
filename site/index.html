Notes

Works best with a keyboard

  1. Copy something

    Select text in the note and press Ctrl + C.

  2. Open Ditto-RS

    Press Ctrl + `, or use the button. Your copy is at the top.

  3. Find and paste

    Type a few letters, pick a clip with ↑ ↓ and press Enter. It lands in the note.

  4. Look around

    Type edinburgh to find the train ticket by the words on it. F3 previews a clip, and a right-click offers Special Paste, groups and sticky clips.

What it does

If you have used Ditto on Windows, you already know how it works. Now it works on your Mac and your Linux machine too.

  • It keeps everything you copy

    Text, formatted text, pictures and lists of files, saved in a history on your own disk. Copy the same thing again and it moves back to the top.

  • You find things by typing

    Start typing and the list narrows. It finds pictures too, by the words in them. Words are combined, and you can add OR, NOT, "exact phrases", wildcards or regular expressions.

  • It pastes where you were

    Pick a clip and Ditto-RS returns to the app you came from and pastes for you. Shift + Enter pastes plain text. Ctrl + 1 to 0 paste the ten newest clips.

  • Special Paste

    Paste in UPPER CASE or lower case, trimmed, on one line, as a web-address slug, or with the time added. Select several clips and paste them together.

  • Keep what matters

    Pin clips to the top, sort them into groups, give them names, and give your favourites their own hotkey that works anywhere.

  • Private by design

    Your history never leaves your computer: no account, no sync, no telemetry. Copies that password managers mark as secret are skipped, and you can exclude any app.

A closer look

See a clip before you paste it

Press F3 to look at the whole clip: the full text, the formatted version, the picture at full size, or the list of files, with where and when you copied it.

Make it yours

Light and dark themes, your own hotkeys, how much history to keep and for how long, which apps to ignore, and where the window opens. Text too small? Turn up the interface size and everything grows at once, up to 300 %. Sensible defaults, taken from Ditto, so you can leave it all alone.

Moving from Ditto on Windows?

Bring your history with you. Options → Database → Import Ditto Database reads your Ditto.db and brings over your clips, groups, sticky clips, quick-paste text and hotkeys.

Notes for Linux

On X11 everything works. Wayland does not let apps register global hotkeys, so bind ditto-rs --show to a shortcut in your desktop's keyboard settings. To search the text in pictures, install Tesseract, for example with sudo apt install tesseract-ocr.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Ditto-RS is free and open source under the GPL-3.0 licence. There is no account, no subscription and no paid version.

Does anything I copy leave my computer?

No. Your history is a file on your own disk, and Ditto-RS has no account, no sync and no telemetry. The source code is public, so you do not have to take that on trust.

How does it find the text in pictures?

Ditto-RS reads the pictures you copy with the text recognition built into your system: Apple Vision on macOS, and Windows' own on Windows. On Linux it uses Tesseract, if you have installed it. The pictures never leave your computer, and you can switch this off in Options.

What about passwords?

Copies that password managers mark as secret are skipped. You can also list apps whose copies should never be saved, and switch saving off for a while from the menu.

Is this the same as Ditto?

Kinda. Ditto is a much-loved clipboard manager for Windows by Scott Brogden. Ditto-RS re-creates it in Rust, working from Ditto's source code and feature list, so that it is fast, lightweight and runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. It works the same way and can import a Ditto database. It is a separate project and is not affiliated with Ditto.

Why does my computer warn me when I open it?

The installers are not signed with a paid Apple or Microsoft certificate yet, so macOS and Windows cannot verify who made them. The app is built in the open from public source code. The steps for the first launch are next to each download.

Where is my history kept?

In %APPDATA%\Ditto-RS on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/Ditto-RS on macOS and ~/.local/share/Ditto-RS on Linux. Put an empty file named portable next to the program to keep everything beside it instead, for example on a USB stick.

Does it work on Wayland?

Mostly. Clipboard history works where the desktop supports the data-control protocol. Wayland does not let apps register global hotkeys or move focus, so you bind ditto-rs --show to a shortcut yourself. On X11 everything works.
